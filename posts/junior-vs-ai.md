# Are Junior Devs useless in the age of A.I.?
It took some time, but by now there is little serious debate about AI replacing senior engineers anytime soon. A number of companies tried — and failed spectacularly. The limitations of AI and its rapidly flattening learning curve have become increasingly clear. Most of us have quietly canceled our plumbing or bricklaying bootcamps.

There’s no denying it: AI’s coding abilities are impressive. For many of us, it has become an incredibly powerful tool that boosts productivity across a wide range of tasks. But its limited context window, its dependence on the skill of the person prompting it, its lack of true uncertainty awareness, and its limited ability to think several steps ahead are just a few of the reasons why it cannot replace an experienced software developer. For the foreseeable future, it will likely remain what it is best suited to be: a highly effective tool.

Yet one group of engineers continues to struggle in the job market. Many argue that AI has made them obsolete: junior developers.

This raises an uncomfortable question: Is the only reason to hire a junior engineer to turn them into a senior one someday? Or do junior developers still have a valuable role to play in today’s AI-augmented world?

### The Doorman Fellacy
The main argument against junior engineers essentially goes like this: "Junior Devs typically get small, well defined tasks.
AI is great at that. Let's get rid of expensive and slow Junior Devs."

This oversimplification of a juniors role within a company reminds me of Rory Sutherland's "Doorman Fellacy": A luxury hotel wants to safe costs by replacing the Doorman with
an automatic door-opener. Surprisingly, after the replacement their numbers go down. After an in depth analysis they realize
the doorman did much more than just opening the door for guests: He carried luggage, provided status to the hotel, 
made guests who arrived late feel safe, made sure the entrance area always looked presentable, ...

Thinking of Junior Developers as coding monkeys who can only work on small, well defined tickets all day long is quite 
far from the reality, and in my opinion highly ignorant. Being bad at coding, unable to think critically or to think ahead has nothing to do 
with being junior. It`s called being bad at your job and this happens on every level.
